Barnaby was a forest gnome who lived inside the hollowed root of an ancient oak tree. While other gnomes kept pets like squirrels or beetles, Barnaby’s best friend was a rock named Jasper.

Jasper was a smooth, river-worn granite stone the size of an apple, speckled with flecks of shiny mica. To anyone else, Jasper was just geology. To Barnaby, Jasper was the finest listener in the entire woodland.

Every morning, Barnaby placed Jasper in the basket of his wooden wheelbarrow and went about his chores. When Barnaby harvested sweet clover, Jasper guarded the basket. When Barnaby sat by the babbling brook to eat his hazelnut spread, he leaned Jasper against a warm moss cushion so his friend could "enjoy the view."

"You're remarkably quiet today, Jasper," Barnaby would say, taking a bite of bread. "I suppose you're thinking about the weather." Jasper, holding steady in the afternoon sun, offered no counterargument.

One stormy evening, a sudden gust of wind knocked Barnaby’s garden fence down, sending a heavy wooden beam rolling straight toward a patch of fragile moonlight lilies. Without thinking, Barnaby threw himself forward to block it, but he tripped on a root.

He didn't hit the ground, though. He landed squarely on Jasper, who happened to be resting right in the root's crook. The small, sturdy granite stone jammed the heavy beam just enough to alter its path, saving both Barnaby and the flowers.

Barnaby sat up, brushing dirt off his pointy hat, and picked up his friend. Jasper had taken a small chip on his side, but otherwise remained as solid as ever.

"A heroic defense!" Barnaby declared, carefully polishing the chip with his sleeve. "I always knew you were a rock in a crisis."
