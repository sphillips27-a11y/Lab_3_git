Barnaby was a forest gnome who lived inside the hollowed root of an ancient oak tree. While other gnomes kept pets like squirrels or beetles, Barnaby’s best friend was a rock named Jasper.

Jasper was a smooth, river-worn granite stone the size of an apple, speckled with flecks of shiny mica. To anyone else, Jasper was just geology. To Barnaby, Jasper was the finest listener in the entire woodland.

Every morning, Barnaby placed Jasper in the basket of his wooden wheelbarrow and went about his chores. When Barnaby harvested sweet clover, Jasper guarded the basket. When Barnaby sat by the babbling brook to eat his hazelnut spread, he leaned Jasper against a warm moss cushion so his friend could "enjoy the view."

"You're remarkably quiet today, Jasper," Barnaby would say, taking a bite of bread. "I suppose you're thinking about the weather." Jasper, holding steady in the afternoon sun, offered no counterargument.

The rock shifted. A tiny, yawn-like rumble resonated from its core, and two glowing golden eyes blinked open through the mica flecks. Stunned, Barnaby froze as four stubby, crystalline legs sprouted from Jasper’s base, followed by a jagged, toothy grin that split the stone wide open.

Jasper wasn't a rock at all. He was a juvenile gargoyle beast, dormant for a century and finally awakened by the warmth of Barnaby’s daily company.

Before Barnaby could scream, a sudden gale blew through the clearing. A heavy wooden beam from the garden fence snapped, hurtling straight toward the terrified gnome.

Jasper let out a surprisingly deep roar. Leaping from his moss cushion, the little beast caught the heavy timber in his stone jaws, snapping the wood in two and landing spryly on his feet. He shook off the splinters, trotted over to Barnaby, and affectionately nudged the gnome's knee.

Barnaby blinked, slowly lowering his hands from his face.

"Well," Barnaby whispered, offering a piece of bread to the creature. "That certainly explains the appetite for pebbles."
